How Does Changing the Slit Separation in a Double-Slit Experiment Affect the Interference Pattern on a Screen?

Equipment

The equipment used in the experiment.
  • Red laser pointer: coherent light source (~650 nm)
  • 6 double-slit slides: slit separations of 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 mm
  • Wall: surface to project the fringe pattern onto
  • 2 metre rulers: measure the 2.00 m distance and provide photo scale
  • Camera: photograph each fringe pattern for measurement
  • Tape: to hold the laser steady at a fixed angle

Variables

Type Variable Detail
Independent (what we change) Slit separation (d) 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 and 3.0 mm
Dependent (what we measure) Pattern width W (cm) and fringe count n Total width of the visible fringe pattern measured from printed photographs with a ruler scale; n = number of visible bright fringes counted per photograph
Controlled (what we keep the same) Distance from slits to screen 2.00 m, fixed with a metre ruler
Laser wavelength Red laser ~650 nm, same device throughout
Lighting Darkened room for all trials
Laser alignment Perpendicular to the slit slide each time
Measurement method Same camera position and ruler scale every time

Procedure

The laser and slit slide setup.

The fringe pattern projected onto the wall screen.
  1. Set up the red laser on a clamp stand aimed at the double-slit slide, with a white screen placed exactly 2.00 m away.
  2. Fully darken the room to allow clear observation of the fringe pattern.
  3. Mark the centre of the pattern on the screen and place a metre ruler horizontally along the screen.
  4. Insert the 0.5 mm slit slide and switch on the laser, aiming it at the midpoint between the two slits.
  5. Take a close-up photograph of the fringe pattern alongside the ruler.
  6. Print the photograph. Count the number of visible bright fringes (n) and measure the total pattern width W (cm) using the printed ruler as a scale reference.
  7. Repeat steps 5–6 five more times for the same slit separation (six trials total).
  8. Repeat the full procedure for each remaining slit separation: 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 and 3.0 mm.
  9. Calculate the average W and n for each slit separation and compare the measured W with the theoretical value W = (λL / d) × (n − 1).
Safety: Never look directly into the laser beam. Secure the laser so it cannot fall or move.
